# Experimental Follow-ups for Researchers

## Checking secondary structure
Circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy estimates helix, sheet and coil content from a protein solution, and is the quickest check of a Chou-Fasman style prediction. FTIR spectroscopy can also detect beta-sheet content.

## Testing aggregation and amyloid formation
Thioflavin T (ThT) fluorescence rises when dye binds amyloid fibrils, so it is the standard kinetic assay for aggregation. Transmission electron microscopy or atomic force microscopy shows fibril morphology. Congo red staining with birefringence is a classic histology test.

## Testing stability
Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) or thermal shift assays (differential scanning fluorimetry) measure the melting temperature. A lower melting temperature after a mutation means destabilization.

## Testing a predicted mutation
Use site-directed mutagenesis to create the variant, express and purify it, then compare it with wild type using CD, ThT and thermal shift. Choose mutations at positions the app highlights, and include a control substitution that should not change behaviour.

## Getting a real structure
X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM or NMR give experimental structures. For a computational check, compare with AlphaFold predictions in the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database and the Protein Data Bank (PDB). Literature searches on PubMed and UniProt entries give known variants and disease links.

## Sensible interpretation
Treat the app's output as a hypothesis generator. Prioritise experiments where several signals agree, for example high beta-sheet score, high hydrophobicity and a high-risk disease pattern together.
