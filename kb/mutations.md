# Point Mutations and Their Effects

## Mutation notation
A point mutation is written as original residue, position, new residue. E22K means the glutamate (E) at position 22 is replaced by lysine (K). Positions count from 1 at the N-terminus. The app's mutate feature accepts this notation and reruns the full analysis on the changed sequence.

## How one substitution changes a protein
A substitution can change hydrophobicity, charge, size or backbone flexibility. Swapping a surface residue for a similar one often does little, but changing a buried hydrophobic residue to a charged one can destabilize the fold. Replacing a residue with proline can break a helix, and replacing glycine can remove needed flexibility in a turn.

## Mutations that raise aggregation risk
Mutations that increase hydrophobicity or beta-sheet propensity in an exposed segment make aggregation more likely. Mutations that remove charge reduce electrostatic repulsion between chains, which also speeds clumping. In amyloid-beta, variants such as E22K and E22G (the Dutch and Arctic types) are linked to early-onset disease because they promote fibril formation.

## Mutations that lower aggregation risk
Introducing charged residues or a proline into an aggregation-prone stretch can disrupt beta-sheet stacking. Researchers use such substitutions as stabilizing controls.

## Reading the mutation result
Compare the before and after values for dominant structure, instability index, hydrophobic ratio, minimum energy and risk score. A large shift in several of them together is more convincing than a change in one number. Always confirm a predicted effect in the lab with site-directed mutagenesis.
